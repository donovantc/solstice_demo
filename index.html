Why does it happen?

Two different effects are happening at once:

1. Daylight is shrinking

As the Northern Hemisphere tilts away from the Sun, the Sun follows a lower, shorter path across the sky.

2. Solar noon is shifting

Because of Earth's orbital motion and axial tilt, the Sun reaches its highest point at a slightly later clock time each day around December.

Sunrise and sunset sit on opposite sides of solar noon. Their midpoint can move later even while the total daylight interval gets shorter.

Sunset = Solar noon + ½ daylight duration

Before the solstice, daylight keeps shrinking. But by mid-December, solar noon is moving later quickly enough to outweigh half the daylight lost. So sunset starts getting later before the shortest day.
